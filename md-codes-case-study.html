Case Study

From a YYZ hotel beta test to a global tour spec.

How DCR Graphics and Productions Inc. — EventStudio's sister company and the recognized brand in the medical aesthetics market — built and refined the live production behind Dr. Maurício de Maio's MD Codes program.

ClientMD Codes / Dr. Maurício de Maio, and Allergan Medical Institute (AMI)
Company of recordDCR Graphics and Productions Inc.
ScopeLive event production, technical spec, before/after live matching, remote-directed filming
Get a quote

Six chapters, one standard

From a hotel meeting room outside Toronto to a specification used on stages in 70+ countries.

01 — The Beta Test

Where MD Codes began

Before Dr. Maurício de Maio's MD Codes technique was a fixture on stages worldwide, it had to start somewhere. DCR Graphics and Productions Inc. produced that start — the very first MD Codes beta test event, held in a hotel meeting room next to Toronto Pearson (YYZ). No world tour yet. No established format. Just a physician with a groundbreaking technique for facial aesthetic treatment, and a production team building the show around him from the ground up.

02 — Earning the Standard

Detail-specific, and unwilling to compromise

De Maio is exact. His requirements for how an event should look, sound, and run are detailed and specific, and the first event was the beginning of a conversation, not the end of one. The team raised its technical spec between his visits — sightlines, camera coverage, screen content, audio — and kept refining across multiple return engagements until the production met the standard he expected every time, everywhere.

03 — The Live Event

Discuss it. Inject it. Reveal it — live.

The live event was the centerpiece of the entire program — everything else supported it. Every MD Codes demonstration runs the same live arc: de Maio discusses what he's about to do, injects the patient live, and reveals the results live — nothing pre-recorded, nothing staged after the fact. Its most technically demanding element was the before/after comparison that capped every demonstration: a patient's "before" photo, taken by a separate vendor ahead of time, had to be matched live on stage by a still frame pulled from the event's own camera feed — same angle, same light, the look frozen at exactly the right instant — so the two images could sit side by side and tell an honest, immediate story of the result, in real time, in front of the room. Getting that match right, live, was the real production challenge behind every show.

04 — From Spec to Global Tour

One document, every market

That refined spec didn't stay in Toronto. The team went on to write the technical specification later used for de Maio's tour across the world — the reference document other markets' production and AV teams worked from to deliver his events, before/after comparison included, at his standard, wherever he appeared.

05 — Andy and "Andeeee"

The relationship that traveled with the show

Part of what made the show work wasn't just technical. Andy Stinton's onstage rapport with de Maio — who calls him "Andeeee" — became part of the identity of the events themselves, the kind of producer-talent trust that shows up on stage and that audiences remember. That relationship helped carry the team's name to global recognition inside the MD Codes world.

06 — The Mentor Program, Filmed Through COVID

Exam-grade filming, directed remotely

When de Maio launched a mentor program for hand-picked top injectors from across Canada, every mentee's exam needed to be filmed — at a moment when almost no live production was happening anywhere. The team designed a remote-capable system: just two technicians on-site setting up video equipment per exam, while Andy Stinton directed the entire shoot remotely from Oakville — operating cameras and talking injectors through their exams in real time, from a distance.
