No. 1

The lumber yard will not design your house

By Andy Stinton, EventStudio

When I tell people I’m in production, they nod politely and assume I make videos. Occasionally they ask if I’ve met anyone famous. I have, but that’s not the point.

In the meetings world, “production” gets used for two quite different jobs. One is AV: supplying and running the screens, microphones, lights and cameras. The other is live event production: designing and running the show itself. That covers the run of show, the speakers and their cues, the timing, the content flow, and who’s in charge when the keynote’s laptop decides to update itself mid-sentence.

Both jobs matter. A good AV company is worth its weight in cable, and no show happens without one. They’re just not the same job, even though they often get sold in the same package.

Other industries sorted this out long ago. A film rental house supplies the cameras, but it doesn’t direct the film. The lumber yard sells the wood, but it doesn’t design the house. In theatre, the lighting supplier isn’t the stage manager. At a wedding, the florist and the DJ are vendors, and the planner is the one who makes them work together. Nobody’s doing anything wrong in any of these cases. They’re simply different trades.

You may have felt the gap without having a name for it: the gear all worked, and the show still felt a bit disorganized. That’s usually a sign nobody was producing it.

Five questions to ask before you sign:

  1. Who writes the run of show?
  2. Who calls the cues on the day?
  3. Who rehearses the speakers?
  4. Who’s in charge if something goes wrong?
  5. What does this quote cover, and what doesn’t it?

If the answer to the first four is “um”, you’ve bought equipment, not a show. That’s perfectly fine, as long as you know it.

I could tell you a dozen stories that began with “I assumed the … would do that.” But that’s another article.

None of this means you need to become an AV expert. I don’t understand room blocks or F&B minimums either, and I’m at peace with that.

Full disclosure: I’m the producer in this story, so I’m biased. But if you’d like a second opinion, contact us. We won’t try to sell you a lumber yard.
