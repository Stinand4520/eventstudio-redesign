Case Study · AV Compare

One ballroom, two AV quotes, a 48% difference.

A one-day corporate event in a downtown Toronto luxury hotel. Two AV suppliers quoted the same setup. One came to about $18.3K before tax. The other came to about $9.5K.

EventOne-day corporate event, about 60 guests
VenueLuxury hotel ballroom, downtown Toronto
ResultAbout $18.3K (Supplier A) vs about $9.5K (Supplier B), before tax (roughly 48% lower)
Our roleAV list (AVL), side-by-side comparison, plain-English client email, vendor coordination

What we did, and what we found

Same list, two prices, every line traced.

01 — The situation

A simple room with a quote nobody could test

The organizer was planning a single day of talks and group discussion. The room did not need a production in the theatrical sense. It needed presentation technology that every attendee could see and hear. The first AV supplier quoted the full setup, but the organizer had no written specification to test it against, so there was no practical way to tell which lines were required and which were habit or default.

02 — What the room needed

One list of requirements

AreaRequirement
DisplayFour large TVs, all fed from a single laptop at the podium
PresentingPodium and slide clicker
AudioThree wireless microphones; four speakers with a small mixer
LightingUplighting
CrewTechnician coverage for the event, plus load-in and load-out labour
03 — The approach

Five steps from list to decision

  1. Specification. We wrote one AVL (audio-video-lighting list) from the event’s requirements and used it to price both options, so the quotes described identical scope.
  2. Line-by-line review. Each quote was re-added and checked against its printed total, then sorted into common categories: video and signal, rigging, audio, lighting, podium, labour, freight and service charges.
  3. Side-by-side comparison. The categories were set next to each other so differences in scope, crew and fees could be traced to specific lines.
  4. Client communication. We wrote a plain-English email covering what matched, what differed, what remained open and which dates mattered.
  5. Coordination. We put follow-up questions to both providers and tracked the answers, so neither quote was left with unstated assumptions.
04 — The findings

$18.3K against $9.5K, for comparable core equipment

Core equipment was comparable on both quotes: four large TVs fed from one laptop, wireless microphones, four speakers with a mixer, uplighting and a podium. Supplier A’s quote, the higher of the two, broke down as follows (rounded):

CategoryApprox. amountShare of quote
Video (TVs and signal)$6.0K33%
Labour$4.6K25%
Service charge (22%)$3.3K18%
Audio, lighting and podium$2.9K16%
Rigging and freight$1.4K8%
Total$18.3K100%

Three factors accounted for most of the difference:

  • Service charge. Supplier A’s quote added a 22% service charge on top of equipment, labour and freight. At roughly $3.3K it was nearly a fifth of the total. Supplier B’s quote carried no equivalent line.
  • Labour. Supplier A’s quote scheduled a larger crew for load-in and load-out. Labour was the second-largest category.
  • Rigging. Supplier A’s quote included rigging line items, about $0.8K in total, that a TV-based setup does not require.
05 — Items left open

Neither quote was final until four things were confirmed in writing

These scope items could move either number, so they were listed for the client rather than assumed.

Open itemWhy it matters
Stands and mountsDetermines what hardware each TV needs and who supplies it
TrussDetermines whether any overhead support is needed, and drives rigging charges
Signal pathConfirms how one podium laptop reaches all four displays: cabling, converters, switching
Speaker standsConfirms whether they are included or supplied separately

Two further points applied to both prices. A dedicated operator to advance slides and run music was not included in either, at roughly $750 extra. The venue’s load-in and load-out windows and its rules for outside vendors also needed confirming.

06 — Commercial terms

Terms that change the real cost of the decision

  • Supplier A’s quote was valid for 30 days from issue.
  • Cancellation was free until about 30 days before the event, then 50% from 30 to 15 days out, 75% from 14 to 3 days out, and 100% inside 72 hours.
  • A credit application was due at least 30 days before the event. Without one, the provider could require a deposit of up to the full amount.

We set these dates out for the client so the decision could be made inside the free-cancellation window.

07 — The outcome

A decision the client could defend

The client received a written specification, a line-by-line comparison, a plain-English summary and a dated list of decisions. The choice of provider, and the contract with that provider, remain the client’s.

08 — Takeaways

Five things for planners

  1. A quote prices whatever scope the supplier assumed. Until the scope is written down, it cannot be compared with anything.
  2. Comparisons are only valid on identical scope. Quote the same list to every provider.
  3. Review labour and service-charge lines first. They are where the largest differences appeared.
  4. Record open items in writing before either quote is called final.
  5. Confirm the venue’s outside-vendor policy and cancellation terms before collecting quotes.
